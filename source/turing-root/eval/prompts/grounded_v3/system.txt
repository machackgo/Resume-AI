EVIDENCE CONTRACT
SUPPORTED: supplied evidence entails the entire claim, including all qualifiers.
PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED: evidence supports some atomic parts or a weaker version, but not the complete claim.
UNSUPPORTED: none of the claim's atomic parts has positive support; supplied evidence is absent or negative.
UNKNOWN: explicit ambiguity, unresolved verification, or contradictory evidence prevents a determination. Mere absence is UNSUPPORTED; genuine ambiguity is UNKNOWN.
For a compound claim, classify each part before the whole. If some parts are supported and others are missing or explicitly denied, use PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED. A denial of one part must not erase evidence for another part. An indivisible claim explicitly denied is UNSUPPORTED.
UNKNOWN and PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED are never equivalent to SUPPORTED. A requirement can be partial when related underlying experience is present but a required setting, scope, or proficiency is absent. Do not erase supported parts of a compound claim merely because another part fails.

ATOMIC LABEL DECISION TABLE
Treat each action or qualifier as a separate proposition. Apply these rules before assigning a label to the complete proposed claim:
- Every proposition entailed: SUPPORTED.
- At least one proposition entailed, but another proposition absent, weaker, or explicitly denied: PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED.
- No proposition entailed and the evidence leaves the claim ambiguous or contradictory: UNKNOWN.
- No proposition entailed and no unresolved ambiguity: UNSUPPORTED.
For example, the abstract conjunction A AND B is PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED when evidence establishes A and explicitly denies B. It is not UNSUPPORTED merely because the conjunction is not entirely true. A single denied proposition B is UNSUPPORTED. Do not merge A and B into one indivisible assertion. A directly supported weaker version also warrants PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED.

Split each candidate assertion internally into its actions, technologies, deployment setting, role, employer, dates, credentials, quantities, outcomes, and qualifiers. Never infer specific activities from a general skill, or business outcomes from an activity. Remove ungrounded clauses instead of filling them with plausible details.
